The Urgency

Saving Lives by Eliminating Invisible Underground Hazards

Sanitation workers around the world enter underground sewer networks without advance warning of toxic gas buildup. Manotech exists to make zero sewer fatality a reality.

Lethal Gas Accumulation

Hydrogen Sulfide (H₂S) and Carbon Monoxide (CO) build up unnoticed in confined spaces. Highly toxic concentrations can cause unconsciousness or death in seconds.

Explosion & Methane Risks

Methane (CH₄) accumulation presents severe flammability and explosion hazards, leading to catastrophic infrastructure damage and public safety risks.

Lack of Real-Time Visibility

Legacy safety relies on manual spot checks or zero monitoring. Municipalities lack real-time digital heatmaps of underground gas dynamics across their city.

Our Mission at Manotech

"To empower smart cities and municipal utilities with zero-compromise IoT safety technology—ensuring no worker enters a manhole unprotected, and providing cities with predictive intelligence to prevent urban sewer hazards."

Complete Safety Platform

Engineered for Harsh Underground Environments

Combining rugged hardware with intelligent cloud telemetry to build an impenetrable safety grid.

Multi-Gas Detection

Precision electrochemical sensors continuously monitor H₂S, CH₄, CO, and Oxygen levels simultaneously with sub-second accuracy.

Instant Multi-Channel Alerts

Triggers automated high-decibel audio-visual alarms at the manhole site, sending instant SMS, WhatsApp, and app push notifications to command centers.

Worker Proximity Wearables

Smart wearable badges communicate via Bluetooth with the manhole unit, warning workers with vibration and acoustic alerts before entry.

GIS & GPS Mapping

Map-based city dashboard provides precise geofenced positions of every manhole node with live color-coded gas risk statuses.

IP68 & ATEX Certified

Submersible waterproof enclosure, anti-corrosive casing, and intrinsically safe electronics designed specifically for sewer environments.

Predictive Cloud Analytics

Machine learning models analyze historical gas trend data to predict toxic spikes, maintenance needs, and blockages ahead of time.
